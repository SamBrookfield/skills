# Costing

Price the described life after the last question and before writing the profile. People underestimate what lives cost, so price realistically and completely.

## Rules

1. Price the life the person described, at the standard they described, in their places. A light-filled high-rise in a capital is priced as one, not as an average flat. Use current market levels you know for those places.
2. When unsure between two levels, take the higher. Missing costs make a life look cheaper than it is.
3. Give one likely figure per item, at today's prices, in the person's currency. State once that the real range is about ±25%. No wide ranges.
4. Round to 2 significant figures.
5. Include the costs people forget:
   - property upkeep (about 1–2% of each home's value a year), insurance and local property taxes
   - staff, cleaners, gardeners or caretakers the life implies, especially for homes empty part of the year
   - transport (cars, drivers, commuting)
   - health cover where it is not free
   - children: childcare, school (state or private as the life suggests), activities
   - savings and pension: 10–15% of spending
   - a 15% buffer for the unexpected
6. For every item, note its basis: what you priced (type, size, area), the rate or market level, and the reason. These notes are shown to the person in chapter 03.
7. Assume typical values for anything never mentioned (e.g. number of children) and mark them with `*` and a one-line assumption.

## Totals

| Total | Rule |
|---|---|
| Per year, after tax | Sum of yearly items, savings and buffer |
| Per year, before tax | After-tax total grossed up with the person's country's income tax, approx. |
| To buy | Sum of one-off purchases (homes, workshop, equipment) |
| Wealth needed | To buy + one year of after-tax spending |
| To live it without working | Only if the person described not working: after-tax total ÷ 0.04 |

## Logistics

Report each that applies in one sentence: can the described day physically happen; who looks after empty homes; does time away fit work and dependants; what the ideal week leaves for everything else.
