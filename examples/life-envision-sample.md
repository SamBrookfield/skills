# Sample output: an expansive profile

A fictional, full-length example of what Life Envision can produce at its best. The person is invented: 31, a product designer in Berlin earning about €62k, renting a one-bed with their partner, about €45k saved, horizon seven years. Each chapter is one message in the chat.

---

**You'll live** on a restored quinta ten minutes inland from Ericeira: a whitewashed main house, four guest rooms around a courtyard, an old stone barn turned into your studio, and a hectare of land running down to a eucalyptus line. You own it. On a clear day you can see the sea from the kitchen.

**You'll work** two ways. Three days a week you're a senior design consultant for two or three clients, mostly remote, with one day a month in Lisbon. It's the same craft you practise now, but you choose the problems and the hours. The other two days belong to the studio: throwing, glazing and firing tableware that ends up on your own guest tables and in a handful of shops in Lisbon and Porto.

**Your people** are close and constant. Your partner runs the guesthouse and loves it. Your daughter is three and spends mornings at the village creche. Guests turn into Friday-night dinners, and neighbours turn into the kind of friends who bring lemons over unannounced.

**You'll feel** unhurried but never idle: full days with your hands and your head, and no Sunday dread.

## 02 / WHAT YOU WANT MOST

**What this life is built around.** Three things carry everything else.
- **Making things with your hands.** A real studio, not a corner of a flat: a 60 m² barn with two wheels, a 1.2 m³ gas kiln and a glaze lab, plus enough output (about 1,500 pieces a year) to sell and to set your own tables.
- **A home that's open.** Four guest rooms booked most of the year, long dinners twice a week, and a house that's never quiet for long. You want people around you without having to go out and find them.
- **The sea at the start of the day.** Surfing before 8am, most days, all year. It's the one fixed point you'd build the rest of the week around.

They fit together unusually well. The guesthouse pays for the land the studio sits on; the studio's work furnishes the guesthouse; the sea is ten minutes from both. Design consulting is the engine that funds the transition and keeps your skills sharp, and you're happy for it to stay at three days for good.

**Trade-offs you made.** You chose Ericeira over Lisbon itself: less culture on the doorstep, but land, the sea and room for a kiln. You let go of the idea of a "big-name studio job" once you saw it was more your old manager's ambition than yours. And you kept consulting rather than going all-in on ceramics, because you'd rather the clay stays a joy than becomes rent.

**Still to picture.** What your partner wants beyond running the guesthouse, and what the quinta looks like in winter, when the guests thin out and the swell is biggest.

All figures are likely estimates at today's prices, accurate to about ±25%.

**How the numbers work.**
- **Quinta:** a 280 m² farmhouse with a stone barn on about a hectare, 5–15 minutes inland from Ericeira, needing work. Coastal demand puts these at about €3,000/m² of house before renovation, so €850k.
- **Renovation:** 160 m² brought up to guest standard (four en-suite rooms and the barn) at about €1,600/m², so €260k.
- **Studio:** a 1.2 m³ gas kiln (about €18k installed), two wheels, a slab roller, glaze lab and ventilation.
- **Running the quinta:** upkeep at 1.5% of the €1.1m property value (€17k), municipal property tax (IMI) at about 0.3% of an assessed value near €500k, and insurance.
- **Guesthouse operations:** laundry, breakfasts, utilities for the guest wing, and the 15% platform commission on bookings.
- **Housekeeper and gardener:** one part-time housekeeper (about 25 hours a week) and a gardener one day a week, at local rates including social security.
- **Before tax:** an effective rate of about 28% across consulting income and guesthouse profit, so €209k ÷ 0.72 ≈ €290k.
- **If you borrowed instead:** a €240k deposit and a €950k mortgage at 4% over 30 years adds about €54k a year. That lowers the wealth you need to about €450k but lifts the yearly figure to about €263k after tax.

**Where the money comes from.** This life partly pays for itself:
- **Guesthouse:** four rooms at €150 a night and 60% occupancy bring in about €131k.
- **Consulting:** three days a week at €650 a day, about 140 days a year, brings in about €90k.
- **Ceramics:** about €18k.

That's about €239k, roughly €50k short of the €290k before tax. The gap closes if occupancy reaches about 75% (the summer months already run above 90% in Ericeira) or consulting goes to four days in winter. Against today, you'd be living on household spending more than three times your current salary. Most of it is funded by the house itself rather than by your job, which is the real shift.

**How it fits together.**
- The surf, the creche run and a 9:30 client call only fit because the beach, the village and the house are all within ten minutes of each other.
- A guesthouse full in summer and a studio full of orders before Christmas pull on the same hands in different seasons. That timing works, as long as it stays that way.
- Two weeks in Mashiko each spring means someone covers the guesthouse, which is easiest in March, before the season starts.

**What it asks of you.** You'd need to learn to run a small hospitality business properly (bookings, licensing, Portuguese tax) and take your kiln work from hobby to production. You'd need people you trust to keep the house running when you're away, and a body that can take dawn surfs and long days at the wheel well into your forties.

## 04 / WHO YOU'RE BECOMING

**In that life, you're someone who…**
- Makes the plates people eat off at your own table, and knows each guest's name by the second morning.
- Does serious design work three days a week and switches it off completely for the other four.
- Checks the swell before the inbox.
- Runs a small, good business on the side without letting it run you.

**You won't trade:** mornings in the sea, the studio days, and dinner as a family.

**You'll know you're living it when…**
- You pull a full kiln load on a Thursday and set six of the pieces on the courtyard table that Friday.
- A returning guest asks for "the room with the blue bowls".
- You realise you haven't opened your laptop on a Monday since March, and nothing has broken.
